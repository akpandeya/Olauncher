- **status**: ingested

## Outline (the book's pedagogical sequence, our content)

1. Presentation — two colour-coded forms tables side by side: indefinite article and
   definite article, each across Nominativ / Akkusativ / Dativ × maskulin / neutrum /
   feminin / Plural; below them the "kein Artikel" block: (a) nouns for things you
   cannot count take no article, (b) `sein` + profession takes no article. Our wording
   rebuilds both tables from our own noun set.
2. Recognition — a picture-plus-noun step: the learner names a thing twice, once as a
   first mention and once as a known one, and sees the article flip between the two.
3. Guided practice — three gap exercise shapes: indefinite article before a direct
   object noun, definite article before a direct object noun (both with the noun and
   its gender given), and a rewrite task that turns negative sentences positive and
   forces the choice between the indefinite article and no article.
4. Use — two A2-flagged text fills: a short narrative in which every thing is first
   introduced (indefinite) and then referred to again (definite); a word bank with the
   articles given supports the weaker rung.
5. Summary — the forms tables themselves plus the implied rule of the word bank:
   first mention indefinite, later mention definite, uncountables bare.

## Rules

### Specific, known noun → definite article (art-gender-fixed, art-gender-natural, art-gender-suffix-*)

- **rule (pre-answer)**: Every noun has one gender, and the definite article follows it. Use the definite article when the listener already knows which one.
- **forms table**:

| | m | f | n | pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nom | der | die | das | die |

- **examples**: `pool:articles` Das Haus ist groß. · `pool:articles` Der Freund ist hier. · `new` Die Lampe ist neu.
- **miss lines**: ✗ die: feminine — the noun is masculine, so der. ✗ das: neuter — the noun is masculine, so der.

The gender-choice knowledge itself is the existing `articles` drill topic and needs no new
items; this lesson only frames it as definite-article usage.

### Indefinite article, first mention (NEW: `art-indefinite-nom`)

- **rule (pre-answer)**: The indefinite article says one of something, or introduces it for the first time. There is no indefinite article for plurals.
- **forms table**:

| | m | f | n | pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nom | ein | eine | ein | – |

The dash is the point: plural nouns stand bare when they are first mentioned.

- **examples**: `new` Das ist ein Pullover. · `new` Eine Tasche ist leer. · `new` Das sind Fotos.
- **miss lines**: ✗ einen: the -en ending belongs to the Akkusativ, not to the subject, so ein. ✗ der: the noun is not known yet, so ein.

### First mention, then again (NEW: `art-first-mention`)

- **rule (pre-answer)**: In a short story, introduce a person or thing with the indefinite article; from then on, the definite article refers to it.
- **forms table**:

| | m | f | n | pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| first mention | ein | eine | ein | – |
| after that | der | die | das | die |

- **examples**: `new` Ein Junge spielt draußen. Der Junge wohnt hier. · `new` Wir sehen eine Katze. Die Katze folgt uns.
- **miss lines**: ✗ der before the first mention: der presumes the thing is known, so ein. ✗ ein after the first mention: the thing is known now, so der/die/das.

### Definite article in the Akkusativ (NEW: `art-definite-akk`)

- **rule (pre-answer)**: A direct object noun stands in the Akkusativ. The definite article keeps its Nominativ shape here except for nouns of one gender.
- **forms table** (Dativ row shown on the spread for the overview; drilled at L17):

| | m | f | n | pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nom | der | die | das | die |
| Akk | den | die | das | die |
| Dat | dem | der | dem | den |

- **examples**: `new` Ich suche den Schlüssel. · `new` Er kauft die Lampe. · `new` Ich repariere das Fahrrad. · `new` Ich wasche die Schuhe.
- **miss lines** (sample item der Schlüssel): ✗ der: the article changes its shape here, so den. ✗ das: das is the form for the other gender and stays the same in both cases, so den.

### Indefinite article in the Akkusativ (NEW: `art-indefinite-akk`)

- **rule (pre-answer)**: Before a direct object noun, the indefinite article takes an ending on one gender and stays the same on the others.
- **forms table** (Dativ row shown on the spread for the overview; drilled at L17):

| | m | f | n | pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nom | ein | eine | ein | – |
| Akk | einen | eine | ein | – |
| Dat | einem | einer | einem | – |

- **examples**: `new` Hast du einen Rucksack? · `new` Er möchte einen Pullover. · `new` Ich brauche eine Tasche. · `new` Wir kaufen ein Fahrrad.
- **miss lines** (sample item ein Pullover): ✗ ein: the form takes -en before this noun here, so einen. ✗ eine: eine belongs to the other gender, so einen.

### Things you cannot count — no article (NEW: `art-null-uncountable`)

- **rule (pre-answer)**: Some nouns name things you cannot count, like time, money or weather. Ask whether the noun is countable in this sentence before choosing an article.
- **forms table** (the pattern is the absence of an article; the row shows the noun standing alone):

| | m | f | n | pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nom | — | — | — | — |

- **examples**: `new` Menschen brauchen Zeit. · `new` Am Ende haben wir Glück. · `new` Geld ist nicht alles.
- **miss lines**: ✗ der / ✗ die / ✗ das / ✗ ein: the noun is not countable here, so it stands without an article.

### Profession after sein — no article (NEW: `art-null-beruf`)

- **rule (pre-answer)**: A profession after sein describes a role, not a specific person. Decide whether this sentence needs an article as a result.
- **forms table** (same shape as the previous rule; the noun stands alone after sein):

| | m | f | n | pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nom | — | — | — | — |

- **examples**: `new` Er ist Koch von Beruf. · `new` Frau Berger ist Ärztin. · `new` Wir sind Studenten.
- **miss lines**: ✗ ein / ✗ eine: after sein, a profession stands without an article. ✗ der / ✗ die: same — the sentence names a role, not one particular person.

## Exercise patterns (shape only)

- **Double frame (recognition)**: for a pictured noun, the learner writes one sentence
  with the indefinite article and a second with the definite article, plus a given
  adjective. Items: nouns drawn from the app pools or written new.
- **Object gap, indefinite**: a question frame with the noun and its gender printed,
  the learner fills the indefinite article. Items: masculine nouns train the -en form;
  feminine and neuter nouns confirm the unchanged forms.
- **Object gap, definite**: the same shape with the definite article and a bracketed
  gender hint after the noun.
- **Negative → positive**: each item turns a negated sentence into a positive one and
  forces the choice between the indefinite article and no article. Uncountables land
  on no article; countable things land on the indefinite article.
- **Two-step text fill (Use)**: a short narrative or city-tour text of six to ten gaps
  where each thing is introduced once and referred to again; the learner alternates
  indefinite then definite. The weaker version supplies a labeled word bank with the
  articles given.

## Drill linkage

- Specific-noun gender choice → existing `articles` rules: `art-gender-fixed`,
  `art-gender-natural`, `art-gender-suffix-ung/-heit/-keit/-schaft/-ion/-taet/-ei/-e/
  -ismus/-ling/-ment/-chen/-lein/-um`, `art-gender-suffix-exception`. These already train
  der/die/das per noun; no new items for rule 1.
- `art-indefinite-nom`, `art-first-mention`, `art-definite-akk`, `art-indefinite-akk`,
  `art-null-uncountable`, `art-null-beruf` — NEW; each needs a `RULES` entry if hints
  are wanted on the later rungs.
- Item-pool additions needed:
  - definite Akkusativ set: der Schlüssel, der Pullover, die Lampe, die Tasche,
    das Fahrrad, die Schuhe (pl).
  - indefinite Akkusativ set: the same nouns with ein-forms, to exercise both shapes.
  - zero-article set: Zeit, Geld, Hunger, Glück, Lust, Musik, Wasser (bare nouns are
    facts, not book text — only the example sentences around them are ours).
  - profession set: Koch/Köchin, Arzt/Ärztin, Lehrer/Lehrerin, Studenten (pl).
  - first-mention pairs for text fills: Junge, Katze, Hund, Tourist, Auto.
