Conventions (author notes, not learner-facing):

- `common_mistakes` (#811): 1–3 short lines, each a typical learner error and the correct form. The card shows them only where no unanswered item is attached — the flashcard and the rules-index detail — never in a pre-answer state or on a later rung. Omitted where no frequent, real mistake exists.
- Layout follows `articles.md`: id, items, rule, first_exposure_rule, forms table, example,
  miss lines, flashcard_and_miss_hint, later_rung_cue.
- No existing ID is reused: `prep-fixed-dat` and `prep-fixed-gen` cover article forms after
  prepositions; the relative pronoun differs where it matters (dative plural *denen*,
  genitive *dessen*/*deren*), so these are different rules.
- `first_exposure_rule` names a case only in `rel-after-prep`, where a one-case preposition

  case is the reasoning step, so it is never named before an answer.
- Forms tables are plain before an answer; the matching cell is marked only in the flashcard
  and after-a-miss states.

## rel-agreement

**id**: `rel-agreement`

**items**:

- Der Mann, ___ dort steht, ist mein Lehrer.
- Die Frau, ___ ich gestern gesehen habe, ist Ärztin.
- Das Buch, ___ ich gerade lese, ist sehr spannend.
- Der Film, ___ wir gestern gesehen haben, war langweilig.
- Die Bücher, ___ auf dem Tisch liegen, gehören mir.

**rule (pre-answer)**: A relative pronoun points back to a noun. Its gender and number copy that noun, but its case is set by its own role inside the relative clause.

**first_exposure_rule**: omitted. The case is the reasoning step here, and the design names a case before an answer only for one-case prepositions.

**forms table**:

| | m | f | n | pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nom | der | die | das | die |
| Akk | den | die | das | die |
| Dat | dem | der | dem | denen |

Equal weight before an answer. After the answer only (flashcard, after a miss): highlight the item's cell and bold its form. The possessive forms (*dessen*, *deren*, distractors in three items) have no row under the 3-row cap; their miss lines explain them.

**example (flashcard and after a miss)**: Das Kind, **das** dort spielt, ist fünf Jahre alt.

**miss lines**:

- Der Mann, ___ dort steht, ist mein Lehrer. (der)
  - ✗ *den*: the object form for a masculine noun; the pronoun is the subject of *steht*, so der.
  - ✗ *dem*: the dative form; *steht* needs no object, so der.
  - ✗ *dessen*: shows possession; the clause has a plain subject, so der.
- Die Frau, ___ ich gestern gesehen habe, ist Ärztin. (die)
  - ✗ *der*: feminine dative or masculine subject — neither fits; *gesehen habe* takes a direct object, so die.
  - ✗ *den*: the masculine accusative form; Frau is feminine, so die.
  - ✗ *deren*: shows possession; the clause has a direct object, so die.
- Das Buch, ___ ich gerade lese, ist sehr spannend. (das)
  - ✗ *dem*: the dative form; *lese* takes a direct object, so das.
  - ✗ *den*: the masculine accusative form; Buch is neuter, so das.
  - ✗ *dessen*: shows possession; the clause has a direct object, so das.
- Der Film, ___ wir gestern gesehen haben, war langweilig. (den)
  - ✗ *der*: the subject form; the pronoun is the object of *gesehen*, so den.
  - ✗ *dem*: the dative form; there is no preposition here, so den.
  - ✗ *denen*: the plural dative form; Film is singular and the object of *gesehen*, so den.
- Die Bücher, ___ auf dem Tisch liegen, gehören mir. (die)
  - ✗ *den*: the masculine accusative form; Bücher is plural and the pronoun is the subject of *liegen*, so die.
  - ✗ *denen*: the plural dative form; the pronoun is the subject of *liegen*, so die.
  - ✗ *das*: the neuter singular form; Bücher is plural, so die.

**flashcard_and_miss_hint**: The noun before the comma sets gender and number; the pronoun's job inside the clause sets its case.

**later_rung_cue**: Gender and number come from the noun; the case comes from the pronoun's own role in the clause.

**common_mistakes**:
- matching the pronoun to the main clause: its gender and number copy the noun — das Kind, das; die Kinder, die.
- the main clause's case for the pronoun: its case follows its own job in the relative clause — den ich sehe, dem ich helfe.

## rel-after-prep

**id**: `rel-after-prep`

**items**:

- Der Mann, mit ___ ich spreche, ist mein Nachbar.
- Die Frau, von ___ ich viel gelernt habe, ist meine Chefin.
- Die Kinder, mit ___ sie im Park spielt, sind sehr nett.

**rule (pre-answer)**: A preposition directly before the relative pronoun decides its case, exactly as with articles. Gender and number still copy the noun it refers to.

**first_exposure_rule**: (flashcard and first tap only) mit and von always take the dative, so the relative pronoun after them uses the dative row, matching the noun's gender and number.

**forms table**:

| | m | f | n | pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Akk | den | die | das | die |
| Dat | dem | der | dem | denen |
| Gen | dessen | deren | dessen | deren |

Equal weight before an answer. After the answer only (flashcard, after a miss): highlight the item's cell and bold its form. No nominative row: a pronoun after a preposition is never nominative.

**example (flashcard and after a miss)**: Der Kollege, mit **dem** ich arbeite, kommt aus Bonn.

**miss lines**:

- Der Mann, mit ___ ich spreche, ist mein Nachbar. (dem)
  - ✗ *den*: the accusative form; mit needs the dative, so dem.
  - ✗ *der*: in the dative row der belongs to a feminine noun; Mann is masculine, so dem.
  - ✗ *dessen*: shows possession; mit needs the plain dative form dem.
- Die Frau, von ___ ich viel gelernt habe, ist meine Chefin. (der)
  - ✗ *die*: the subject or direct-object form; von needs the dative, so der.
  - ✗ *den*: the masculine accusative form; Frau is feminine, so der.
  - ✗ *deren*: shows possession; von needs the plain dative form der.
- Die Kinder, mit ___ sie im Park spielt, sind sehr nett. (denen)
  - ✗ *den*: after mit the plural relative pronoun is denen — den is only the plural article form, not the relative pronoun.
  - ✗ *die*: the subject or direct-object form; mit needs the dative, so denen.
  - ✗ *deren*: shows possession; mit needs the plain dative form denen.

**flashcard_and_miss_hint**: mit and von are dative prepositions; the plural dative relative pronoun is denen.

**later_rung_cue**: After a preposition, the preposition — not the clause — decides the pronoun's form.

**common_mistakes**:
- moving the preposition out of the clause: die Freundin, mit der ich reise — the preposition stays right before the pronoun.
- dem for every gender after mit: the dative form follows the noun's gender — mit der Sängerin, mit dem Kollegen.

## rel-genitive

**id**: `rel-genitive`

**items**:

- Der Mann, ___ Auto in der Garage steht, ist Polizist.
- Die Frau, ___ Sohn in meiner Klasse ist, kommt aus Köln.

**rule (pre-answer)**: A noun right after the relative pronoun marks possession (his car, her son). Its possessive form matches the owner noun the clause refers back to, not that noun.

**first_exposure_rule**: omitted. The case is the answer's case here, and the design names a case before an answer only for one-case prepositions.

**forms table**:

| | m | f | n | pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| possession | dessen | deren | dessen | deren |

Equal weight before an answer. After the answer only (flashcard, after a miss): highlight the item's cell and bold its form. The row label avoids naming the case.

**example (flashcard and after a miss)**: Der Mann, **dessen** Auto rot ist, ist mein Nachbar.

**miss lines**:

- Der Mann, ___ Auto in der Garage steht, ist Polizist. (dessen)
  - ✗ *dem*: the dative form used after prepositions; Auto shows possession, so dessen.
  - ✗ *den*: the masculine accusative form; Auto shows possession, so dessen.
  - ✗ *deren*: the possessive form for a feminine or plural noun; Mann is masculine, so dessen.
- Die Frau, ___ Sohn in meiner Klasse ist, kommt aus Köln. (deren)
  - ✗ *die*: the subject or direct-object form; Sohn shows possession, so deren.
  - ✗ *der*: the masculine subject or feminine dative form; Sohn shows possession, so deren.
  - ✗ *denen*: the plural dative form used after prepositions; Sohn shows possession, so deren.

**flashcard_and_miss_hint**: dessen is "his" for a masculine or neuter noun; deren is "her" for a feminine or plural noun.

**later_rung_cue**: When a noun without its own article follows the pronoun and belongs to the noun it refers to, the clause shows possession: use the special possessive form.

**common_mistakes**:
- sein or ihr for the possessed noun: German uses dessen/deren — der Junge, dessen Hund.
- dessen for every owner: dessen for masculine and neuter owners, deren for feminine and plural — die Lehrerin, deren Kurs.
